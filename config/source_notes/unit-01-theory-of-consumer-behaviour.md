# Source notes — Theory of Consumer Behaviour

These notes support introductory consumer-choice concepts. They do not establish official MPPSC syllabus alignment.

## Price and income effects

OpenStax, *Principles of Economics 2e*, section 6.2, explains that a price change can affect consumption through both a substitution effect and an income effect. The income effect reflects the change in real purchasing power when a price changes while nominal income is held constant. For a normal good, the usual income effect of a price rise reduces quantity consumed; for an inferior good, the income-effect direction can differ.

Source: https://openstax.org/books/principles-of-economics-2e/pages/6-2-how-changes-in-income-and-prices-affect-consumption-choices

OpenStax key terms also distinguish the substitution effect from the income effect and define the income effect as a purchasing-power change. 
Source: https://openstax.org/books/principles-of-economics-2e/pages/6-key-terms

## Giffen goods

A Giffen good is a special inferior good for which the negative income effect is sufficiently strong to outweigh the substitution effect, so quantity demanded can rise when its own price rises. This is an exceptional case, not a general reversal of the law of demand for ordinary goods.

MIT OpenCourseWare, *Principles of Microeconomics*, discusses the condition that the good must be inferior and the income effect must dominate the substitution effect. It also cautions that the familiar Irish potato famine illustration is not settled empirical proof.
Source: https://ocw.mit.edu/ans7870/14/14.01SC/MIT14_01SCF11_rttext.pdf

SATHEE (IIT Kanpur), *Theory of Consumer Behaviour*, gives the same core condition: the good is inferior and its income effect outweighs the substitution effect.
Source: https://sathee.iitk.ac.in/sathee-ssc/student-corner/ncert-books/class-12/economics/introductory-to-microeconomics/chapter-02-theory-of-consumer-behaviour/

## Scope and editorial safeguards

- State the law of demand as a ceteris paribus relationship between a good's own price and quantity demanded. Do not present perfect information as a universal assumption of the law itself.
- Information assumptions belong to a specified model. OpenStax section 16.1 distinguishes imperfect information from asymmetric information; asymmetric information means one party has more relevant information than another.
  Source: https://openstax.org/books/principles-of-economics-2e/pages/16-1-the-problem-of-imperfect-information-and-asymmetric-information
- Do not assert a precise Alfred Marshall *Principles of Economics* (1895) attribution for the Giffen label or treat the Irish example as conclusive unless a direct historical source is supplied and checked.
- Keep Hicks/Slutsky decomposition within the consumer-theory discussion; state that textbook decomposition as a framework, not as a universal explanation covering every theory of demand.
- Distinguish Giffen goods from Veblen/status goods; a status-related demand story is not the defining Giffen condition.
- The examples in a lesson may be hypothetical. Label them as such and do not imply they are empirical observations.
